# A business hypothesis for Aventier

Aventier's first customer hypothesis is a school or careers-advice team helping learners make a career choice and follow through. The learner's immediate benefit is a clear comparison, an evidence trail and a next action they can discuss with an adviser. The pilot must establish whether that benefit is useful often enough for an organisation to pay.

## What exists and what remains a hypothesis

The demo combines guided discovery, three weighted options, learner ranking, a locally saved plan, pathways, curated opportunities, source views, historical evaluation and a prepared evidence guide. It requires no account or live conversational AI. The launch catalogue contains 412 evidence profiles; interest matching covers 20 roles. Missing data, preliminary methods and limited route coverage are product constraints.

Adviser dashboards, cohort reporting, school accounts, assignment workflows, institution integrations and cross-device sharing are proposed extensions. They are not presented as shipped features. There are no claimed customers, signed pilots, revenue, retention figures or measured educational outcomes in this handoff.

## A narrow first pilot

Propose a four-to-six-week pilot with two or three schools or adviser teams, subject to their agreement. These are recruitment targets, not existing partners. Start with one year group and a supported set of career directions rather than promising personalised recommendations across the whole catalogue.

1. Observe a baseline advice session and agree what counts as a useful learner plan.
2. Let a learner complete the flow, inspect sources and take the export to an adviser.
3. Have the adviser check route suitability, missing evidence and the proposed next action.
4. Revisit the learner's plan after an agreed interval and ask what was completed or changed.
5. Review the results with the school and decide whether to continue, revise the product or stop.

Agree the evaluation and decision thresholds before the pilot. Measure completion and return visits, whether learners understand the evidence limits, whether plans contain a specific feasible action, action follow-through and adviser preparation time. Collect reasons for abandoning or changing a recommendation. Compare against the team's usual workflow where practical; self-reported confidence alone cannot establish that a career choice is better.

Because the intended learner can be 17, the pilot needs clear school responsibilities, age-appropriate explanations, accessible participation and agreed handling of any identifiable information. The current local-only plan avoids creating a central learner-profile database. Any sharing or accounts should be deliberately introduced with clear consent, access and deletion controls.

## Revenue and pricing to test

Keep individual learner entry free. Test an organisational subscription for an adviser workflow that helps review plans and follow up across a cohort. Avoid incentives tied to steering a learner toward a paid course or employer.

**Untested pricing assumption:** £1,000–£2,000 per school per year for the proposed adviser workspace, onboarding and cohort follow-up. This is a discovery range, not a market benchmark, offer or validated willingness to pay. Agree a pilot price separately and record what service it includes. Do not charge for features that have not been delivered.

Customer interviews must establish who owns the budget, what the purchase replaces, procurement requirements and what evidence would justify renewal. A paid pilot and a renewal decision provide stronger evidence than a positive demo reaction. No market size is asserted without a defensible source and a defined buyer population.

## Why this could scale

The opportunity is a repeated advice workflow: discover a direction, compare evidence, agree an action and revisit the plan. If schools value it, distribution could follow adviser networks and introductions from pilot teams. These routes need testing; no partnerships are assumed.

The public datasets are not a proprietary moat. A defensible product would need reliable occupation mapping, visible provenance, maintained routes, human-labelled evaluation and a workflow advisers repeatedly choose to use. Expand matching beyond 20 roles only when coverage and evaluation justify it; a searchable evidence profile alone is insufficient.

Default prepared guidance avoids requiring a live-model call for every interaction. That does not establish attractive margins. Track data maintenance, source-licence review, hosting, support, school onboarding, accessibility work and any optional AI usage before making unit-economics claims.

## Decisions before expansion

| Question                                                  | Evidence needed                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Do learners leave with a usable decision and next action? | Adviser-reviewed plan quality, evidence comprehension, observed follow-through and documented failure cases.         |
| Will schools use it again?                                | Repeat adviser use, a clear budget owner, paid-pilot demand and a renewal decision.                                  |
| Can matching safely cover more roles?                     | Human-labelled held-out evaluation, subgroup/error review, source completeness and suitable training-route coverage. |
| Can a shared adviser product earn trust?                  | Clear access and deletion behavior, reliable exports, accessible sessions and transparent limits.                    |
| Can the service operate sustainably?                      | Measured delivery/support costs, resolved redistribution rights and tested pricing.                                  |

The next business milestone is an agreed pilot with a measurable decision rule. Funding a larger product would require evidence of repeat use and a paying buyer, alongside responsible handling of learners and demonstrably useful recommendations.
